## 008 · I talk for 2 minutes, AI plans my whole week
**Pillar:** ⚙️ Workflow Rebuild · **Hook text:** "2-minute voice memo. Whole week planned." · **Keywords:** weekly planning, productivity hacks, chatgpt voice, to do list

### Voiceover
> Every Monday I talk to my phone for two minutes, and AI turns it into my entire week.
> If your to-do list lives in your head, this is the easiest system you'll ever set up.
> Step one. Open voice mode in ChatGPT, Gemini or Claude, or just record a voice memo and use your phone's transcription. Then brain dump. Everything. Deadlines, errands, that email you keep avoiding. Don't organise it. Just talk.
> Step two. Send it this prompt: "Turn my brain dump into a plan for the week. Group tasks by project. Mark anything with a deadline. Put the three most important tasks first. Then suggest which day to do each one."

> Bonus: at the end of the week, paste in what you actually got done and ask what should move to next week. It's like having an assistant who never judges you.

## 009 · Airlines hope you don't know this rule
**Pillar:** 💸 AI Money Moves · **Hook text:** "Airlines hope you don't know this" · **Keywords:** flight cancelled refund, airline refund, travel hacks

> **Research note (do before recording):** check the current rules at transportation.gov/airconsumer. The core rule (in the US, a refund when the airline cancels or significantly changes your flight and you don't accept the alternative) is long-standing, but confirm the details are unchanged. For a UK/EU audience, swap in UK261/EU261.

### Voiceover
> Airlines hope you don't know this rule, because it means they owe you cash, not a voucher.
> In the US, if your flight gets cancelled or significantly changed, and you don't accept the alternative they offer, you're entitled to a refund to your original payment method. Not credit. Your money. It's a Department of Transportation rule.
> But you still have to ask the right way. So let's have AI write it.
> Step one. Gather the basics: your confirmation number, the flight number, the date, and what happened.
> Step two. Paste this into any AI chatbot: "My flight [number] on [date] was cancelled and I did not accept the rebooking. Write a short, firm refund request to [airline] citing the Department of Transportation refund rules. Ask for a refund to my original payment method, not a voucher."
> Step three. Read it, then send it through the airline's refund form and save a copy.
> Quick check before you send: make sure it didn't invent any rule numbers. AI sometimes does.
> If they only offer a voucher, you can file a complaint on the Department of Transportation's website.
> Bonus: in the UK and the EU, the rules can be even stronger. You may be owed compensation on top. Ask AI which rules apply to your flight.
> Follow, because tomorrow I'll show you how to make AI stop sounding like AI.

## 010 · Make AI stop sounding like AI
**Pillar:** ✍️ Prompt Fix · **Hook text:** "If AI keeps sounding like AI…" · **Keywords:** chatgpt writing, humanize ai text, chatgpt tips

### Voiceover
> If your AI writing keeps sounding like AI, you know, "delve," "in today's fast-paced world," here's the fix.
> The problem isn't the tool. It's that it doesn't know how you write. So show it.
> Step one. Find something you've written that sounds like you. An email, a long text, a post. Two or three paragraphs is enough.
> Step two. Paste it in with this: "Here's a sample of my writing. Describe my style in five bullet points. Tone, sentence length, words I use, and what I avoid."
> Step three. Now add a ban list: "Write [the thing you need] in my style. Don't use these words: delve, crucial, landscape, elevate, unlock, seamless. No intro sentence. Start with the point."
> Before, and after. Shorter sentences, my words, and it gets straight to the point.
> Save that style description somewhere. Next time, paste it in first, and you can skip steps one and two completely.
> Bonus: in ChatGPT and Claude you can save it once in your settings, as custom instructions or a style, so every new chat already writes like you.
> Follow, because tomorrow I'll build a full budget spreadsheet in sixty seconds, without typing a single formula.

### Shots

2. **2–8s:** Cringe AI email, zoom on the clichés.
3. **8–16s:** Your writing sample (a real email of yours, **blurred** names).

> Bonus: set your alert just below the lowest price from the last three months. That's usually the real deal price.

## 014 · Stop re-reading your notes. Make AI quiz you.
**Pillar:** ✍️ Prompt Fix · **Hook text:** "Stop re-reading your notes." · **Keywords:** study tips, active recall, study with ai, exam prep

### Voiceover
> Stop re-reading your notes. It's one of the least effective ways to study, and AI can fix it.
> Memory research keeps finding the same thing: testing yourself beats re-reading. It's called active recall, and AI makes the perfect quiz partner.
> Step one. Paste your notes, a chapter, or your lecture slides into any AI chatbot.
> Step two. Use this prompt: "Quiz me on this material. Ask one question at a time and wait for my answer. If I'm wrong, explain why in two sentences, then ask a follow-up on the same idea. Get harder as I get things right. Keep score."
> Step three. Answer from memory. No peeking.
> Watch. It asks, I answer, and when I get one wrong, it explains why and hits me with a follow-up on the same idea.
> Ten minutes of this sticks better than another hour of highlighting.
> Bonus: at the end, say "list the topics I got wrong and make me flashcards for them." Paste them into a flashcard app, and your revision plan is done.
> This works for exams, job interviews, a new language, even learning your company's product.
> Follow, because Shortcut number fifteen drops tomorrow, and it's one you'll use every day.

### Shots
1. **0–2s:** Highlighter-covered notes → big lime ❌; hook box.
2. **2–10s:** Text on screen: "Re-reading ❌ · Testing yourself ✅".
3. **10–16s:** Paste notes into the chatbot.
4. **16–30s:** Prompt typed.
5. **30–46s:** Live quiz: question → your typed answer → "Not quite, here's why…" → follow-up. Score counter.
6. **46–56s:** Flashcards prompt → neat Q/A list.
7. **56–64s:** Icons: exam, interview, language, work.
8. **64–70s:** SHORTCUT #014 tag + "Shortcut #015 tomorrow".
